Questions parents ask

General information. Rules can differ by state and district.

What is an IEP?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a written plan for a child who qualifies for special education services at a U.S. public school, under a federal law called the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). It describes your child's needs, goals, services and supports.

Who is the kit for?

Parents and caregivers of children who have, or may need, an IEP at a U.S. public school. That includes families of autistic children, children with ADHD, learning disabilities and other needs.

How do I ask the school to evaluate my child?

You can ask in writing. Many parents send a dated letter or email to the principal or special education director and keep a copy.

How long does the school have to evaluate my child?

Under IDEA, the initial evaluation generally must be finished within 60 days after you give consent, unless your state sets a different timeline. Check your state education department.

Do I have to agree with everything at the meeting?

No. You can ask questions, ask for changes and ask for time to review before you sign. If you still disagree, IDEA describes options such as mediation, a state complaint or a due process hearing. The school must give you a notice that explains your rights.

Does it work in every state?

The kit follows the general IEP process under federal law, so it can help in any state. States and districts have their own forms and timelines, so check your state education department for specifics.

Is this legal advice?

No. The kit is an educational resource. It does not replace advice from a licensed attorney or advocate, and it does not guarantee any outcome from your school.
